## About Me

I am a rising 4th year Ph.D. student in Informatics at Penn State, advised by [Dr. James Z. Wang](https://wang.ist.psu.edu/docs/home.shtml). I am also a Research Scientist Intern at Meta Reality Labs Research for the Summer and Fall of '26, working with [Dr. Michael Proulx](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=oU9uSvYAAAAJ&hl=en). My research lies at the intersection of human-centered and human-inspired AI, with a particular focus on affect and cognition. I study how AI can develop a richer understanding of the subjective world that humans inhabit, and how computational models can help us understand the cognitive and affective processes underlying human experience. 

One direction of my work examines how well AI systems capture human affect and cognition. I study multimodal models' understanding of emotions expressed through body language and other nonverbal cues, the cognitive processes underlying emotional responses, and the social and cultural norms that shape how emotions are expressed and interpreted. I use these models both to evaluate AI's understanding of human behavior and to probe the computational structure of affect and cognition.

The other direction asks how insights from human cognition can enrich AI systems. For example, I draw on psychological theories of cognition and appraisal to develop human-inspired approaches to self-assessment and uncertainty in foundation models. More broadly, I investigate how principles underlying human judgment and reasoning can inform the design of AI systems with richer forms of cognition.

Ultimately, I see these directions as a closed loop for studying intelligence. Understanding how humans perceive, feel, and reason can inspire new computational principles for AI, while increasingly capable AI systems can serve as models and tools for probing intelligence itself. My long-term goal is to use this interplay between understanding humans and building AI to develop a deeper computational account of intelligence and subjective intelligence—its cognitive and affective foundations, its manifestations, and the principles that allow intelligent systems to make sense of the world.

Prior to joining my PhD, I was a Software Engineer at [Microsoft, India](https://www.microsoft.com/en-in/msidc/), working with Azure storage. I graduated with a B.Tech. in Computer Science from IIEST Shibpur in 2022. 

As an undergraduate student, I was fortunate to have been advised by [Prof. Chris Biemann](https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/en/inst/ab/lt/people/chris-biemann.html) and [Dr. Abhik Jana](https://www.iitbbs.ac.in/profile.php/abhik/) at the University of Hamburg, for my internship as a DAAD WISE Scholar. I worked on improving lexical resources for low-resource languages during this internship. For my undergraduate thesis at IIEST, I worked on applications of graph learning, and explored areas like medical diagnosis using GNNs, and social network analysis.

Besides academics, I love music in any and every form. I trained in Indian classical and folk music vocals for almost 2 decades, often performing professionally in India. I also enjoy theatre, and was part of a professional group for over 6 years. I also love reading and writing (without AI, in an inefifficient manner). I have also come to believe that I love struggling at new activities (especially sports), and that this as a general habit has a positive payoff when doing open-ended research!
